01
The pain

You have never been able to say: “you may spend this much, and I can stop you instantly.”

Hand over a card number and you hand over everything the card can do. Cancel a subscription and you fight a form. Give an AI assistant your details and you are trusting an app’s promise, not a rule. Every payment instrument we have binds authority to a credential — so whoever you cut off simply gets a new one.

03
Why it is structural, not sloppy

Authority binds to the instrument. People are not instruments.

CardsAuthority is the number. Steal the number, become the customer. Cancel the card, the merchant re-onboards.
Bank mandatesDirect debits are revocable by form, chargebacks by dispute — both slow, both after the money left.
Token allowancesApprove an address. It spends. Revoke it, and the same operator deploys a fresh address in one transaction.

And now the counterparty is software that can spawn a new address in a second, forever, for free.

04
The solution

Bind the authority to the human behind the machine.

World already answers “is there one unique person behind this wallet?” — the AgentBook registry maps an agent address to an anonymous human identifier, on-chain. We read that registry from inside a payment contract and enforce every rule against the person:

The budget is theirsTwo of their agents share one rolling 24-hour cap. A new address is not a new allowance.
The revocation is theirsCut them off and every agent they will ever operate loses access in the same transaction.
Widening costs a faceSpending inside the cap needs nobody present. Raising it, or moving the payee, needs a fresh Selfie Check.

07
How it is built

160 lines of Solidity and one registry read.

Contract

Solidity 0.8.26, Foundry. No owner, no pause, no proxy, no upgrade path — nobody, including us, can reach a user’s funds. Custody never moves: the contract holds an allowance and can only ever pay the recipient fixed at authorisation. Rolling 24-hour window, per-payment ceiling, EIP-712 step-up with dynamic linking to amount and payee.

Identity

World AgentBook read directly from Solidity through a pluggable registry interface, so the same mandate works against any person-registry. The raw World ID nullifier never touches the chain — the payer authorises a reference scoped to this chain and this contract, so nothing here becomes a cross-context handle.

Liveness

Selfie Check verified server-side against World’s Developer Portal; the backend signs an EIP-712 authorisation and never sends a transaction. The user submits their own escalation.

Tests

40 passing, including fork tests that read World’s real AgentBook on mainnet at a pinned block. Adversarial cases included: replayed proof, proof for another payee, broken registry, cap reset across midnight.

10
What we know is still wrong

The honest list, before you find it.

Personhood raises the price of a clean identity — it does not stop respawn.AgentBook’s register() overwrites its mapping with no consent check, so one cooperating verified person can re-bind a new address. We read the deployed source. The cost of a fresh identity goes from zero to roughly the price of renting someone’s verification — real, but finite.
Step-up over a plain wallet guards a door in a building with no walls.Whoever steals the payer’s key can simply transfer the tokens. This becomes airtight only when the payer is a policy-restricted smart account that may call this contract and nothing else. That is the next module, and we would rather say it than be asked it.
World App revokes token approvals after every transaction.There is no standing-allowance primitive inside a mini-app today, so the payer must be an ordinary wallet. This is the single largest structural gap for agent commerce in the ecosystem, and it is in our feedback to World.
The demo asset is a test token.The enforcement is real, on mainnet, with real gas. The thing being moved is not money you would miss.
